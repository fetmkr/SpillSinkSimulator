Two things this changes.

The structure is worth about 2.1×, not the 5–30× ρdh credits it with. ρdh is a hemispherical total — the fraction of the beam that leaves in any direction. At the audience the panel buys 0.0078 → 0.0037 over its own coating. The difference is not error: a honeycomb is a retroreflector, so much of what ρdh scores as removed is in fact redirected back up the beam. Real light, going somewhere the audience is not.

It does not beat black velour. Velour reads β = 0.0032 over this room’s angles against the panel’s 0.0037. And the velour figure is already generous: it is a Lambertian model, and its own source reports velour with the lowest TIS of any sample it measured, meaning velour is not Lambertian. The case for this panel is not that it out-darkens velour — it is that it is a rigid ceiling panel with a buyable process, at velour’s brightness.

Weighted over the cells this room actually uses — every projector, every part of the scan field, every place a person may stand. The two Lambertian references are the check on the rig: through the identical 48-cell grid, white paper returns 0.800001 and black velour 0.002000, so the rig gives back exactly what it is handed and anything the panel reads is the panel. A check that failed its own predictions

Was the ranking an artefact of sample size?

Every design was measured on one 60 mm panel, so the number of cells in the sample falls as the pitch rises — 30 at pitch 2, under 4 at pitch 16. GATE 11 of the rig audit swept 5/10/25/50 cells and found ρdh falling monotonically and still falling at 50, with a ten-cell sample reading about 5 % high. The spread across pitch here is about 3 %. The bias was larger than the effect, so the ranking was not quotable and the top twelve were re-measured at a constant 25 cells a side.

Where the light goes

Excellent for the audience, and a retroreflector

A honeycomb wall and a flat floor are mutually perpendicular mirrors, so a bounce off both reverses the ray: the brightest structure on every map is the retro diagonal. It is superb in front of people and it sends light back up the beam. Whether that matters depends on where the projectors sit relative to the audience, and nothing in this study knows that yet.

The room

The audience plane — which corner of the map this room uses

The panel has a reading for every incidence against every observation angle. A room only ever visits part of it, and this one visits a corner that the search never scored. The geometry below is stated, not measured — a 6 m ring of projectors at 1.6 m aimed inward and up at 45° with a ±25° square scan field, a ceiling at 4.5 m, and an audience keeping inside 1.5 m. Everything after it is arithmetic.

The band the panel was chosen in is not the band this room uses. Scoring is θ = 0/±20/±40. This rig delivers 20–70°, so 68 % of what it throws at the ceiling arrives outside the scored band — and normal incidence, where the panel is far and away at its best, never happens at all. That is README open item 1, and the geometry above closes it.

What would change these numbers

The search is conditional on a material nobody has measured

anodised_hi is an estimate in every shape parameter. The library marks its diffuse fraction, roughness and refractive index estimated — translated from Kaster 2025, with its own note reading “Sweep it; do not trust it.” ρ0 = 6 % is the pessimistic end of a 3–6 % spread. A goniometer reading of the actual foil would settle it and could move every absolute number on this page.

  • The coating model is not reciprocal, so the BRDF cells carry that caveat. The structure of the maps is geometric and stands.
  • Zero physical measurements. Still true of the whole study, and still the largest open item.
  • One azimuth plane. φ was not swept.
  • The maps cover the scoring band, ±40°, not grazing — margin 2.0 is measured-good there and a 6.5-depth skirt on an 80 mm cell is a frame five times wider than the sample.
