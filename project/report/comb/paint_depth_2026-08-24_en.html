How far down does the black have to go

9.53 mm cell · 40 mm deep · 0.08 mm foil. The honeycomb a supplier said could not be painted black on the inside, and could not be anodised either. Both are true of their line. Neither turns out to matter much.

Verdict

The deep wall never needed painting. For light arriving head-on, the foil rim and the backing plate decide almost all of it, and both are flat exposed faces. Only oblique light uses the wall, and it stops mattering 15 mm down.

A honeycomb cell is a trap that opens one way

The strangest number in the table is that head-on is five times darker than oblique. The reason is on the way out, not the way in.

Light arriving straight down never touches a wall and reaches the floor. Getting out is the problem. From the bottom of a 40 mm well the exit subtends a half-angle of only 6.8 degrees, and of the light scattered there just 1.4 % leaves in one go. The rest hits a wall, and loses most of itself each time.

Oblique light at 40 degrees meets a wall 5.7 mm down. It never reaches the floor. From that shallow point the exit is open to 40 degrees and 41 % simply leaves — 30 times the head-on share.

That last drop is not gloss. The two coatings have the same diffuse fraction (0.993 against 0.99) and the same roughness. It is Musou being five times darker, showing through.

Thin foil is not a special order. Hexcel grades its core as "cell size – alloy – foil gauge", and 3/8-5052-.001 is a catalogue part: 3/8 inch (9.53 mm) cell, 0.001 inch (0.0254 mm) foil. The 0.08 mm we had been assuming is on the heavy side for this cell.

There is one axis a tube cannot buy. The smear target is 1.42 and a honeycomb sits near 0.96 at every depth, cell size and foil gauge. A flat plate is 1.0 by definition, so the honeycomb does not blur the shape at all. If that axis matters, a tube is not the answer.

How it can be made

1. Paint the backing plate separately and bond afterwards

Half the head-on return and a factor of six on flash live on that plate, and it is flat. Paint it before the core goes on and the depth problem never arises.

2. Spray only 15 mm from the top

40 mm does not have to be coated. The aspect ratio to reach is 1.6 to 1, not 4.2 to 1. That sprays.

3. Dip it

An automotive catalyst substrate is a 1 mm channel 100 mm long — 100 to 1 — and it is dip-coated to a uniform 30 µm with no cracking at the corners. Ours is 4.2 to 1, twenty-four times easier. If pooling is the worry, dip-spin throws the excess off centrifugally.

[verified] cordierite honeycomb dip coating · Chem-Plate dip-spin

4. Electrocoat (ED, e-coat)

Dipping with a field behind it. As the outside coats it insulates, and the current moves to whatever is still bare, so blind holes and internal cavities come out even. The Faraday cage that stops powder coating does not apply. 25–50 µm. But the finish comes out smooth, and smooth means gloss, and gloss raises the flash. Any face that shows has to be finished matte separately.

[verified] Valence: e-coat vs powder coat

5. Coat the foil before expanding it

Honeycomb is made by printing adhesive lines on flat foil, stacking and pressing it into a block, slicing, and expanding. Treating the surface before the block is standard. Done this way there is no depth at all.

[verified] Corex honeycomb manufacturing

6. A slotted grid instead of a honeycomb

Notched strips pushed together. Every blade is flat before assembly, so paint it flat and then build. Depth is just blade height. The cells come out square, so our measurements would have to be repeated.

[verified] Edee eggcrate louvers

Black anodised aluminium and ordinary matte black paint are the same to within half a percentage point. Anodising was never the good answer.

And the supplier is right on the physics. Black anodising runs 10–25 µm and about half of it grows inward, eating the metal. On 80 µm foil, 25 µm a side leaves 55 µm of metal and the rest is brittle oxide. The fold lines would not survive expansion.

[verified] Xometry, black anodising thickness

Questions for the supplier

  • Can you supply the backing plate separately? We will paint it and bond the core on afterwards.
  • We need black only 15 mm down from the top, not through the full 40 mm.
  • Do you have a partner who dips or electrocoats?
  • Can the foil be coated black before it is expanded?
  • Does 0.04 mm foil stand up at 40 mm depth? Hexcel lists 3/8-5052-.001 at 0.0254 mm as a catalogue part, but we have not confirmed it at this depth.
  • Anodising is not needed. Matte black paint is equivalent, provided the gloss is low.
