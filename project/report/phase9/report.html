Manufacturing physics: what 100 panels of 1 m² may and may not be made of

The user needs ~100 units of 1 m². At that quantity two simulation-answerable questions dominate the price: can the panel be EXTRUDED as an endless groove profile, and can the Musou paint be SKIPPED on non-critical zones. Both are now measured. Predictions were registered in scripts/sweep_phase9.py before rendering; gradings live in results/FINDINGS_phase9.md. Anchor P5_j00 reproduced the book value to all digits.

Answer 1 — extrusion: a tip LINE is 40× worse than a tip POINT

The pyramid’s head-on cost is quadratic in tip/pitch: its 0.1 mm flat is a point, 0.06 % of area. An extruded groove’s tip land is a LINE — the same 0.1 mm costs 2.5 % of area, 40× more flat land looking straight back at the audience. Measured at the 7.5 mm deployment beam: head-on 0.894 at the worst orientation vs the pyramid’s 0.040 — ×22, exactly the predicted-in-hindsight class, and the registered prediction (0.040 ± 0.020) missed by that factor, which IS the finding. Extrusion dies also wear tips round first, so the land only grows over a production run.

Answer 2 — bare black obeys total = 0.18 × ρ

Unpainted Lambertian pyramids measured linear over ρ 0.04–0.08 with escape factor 0.18–0.185 — and the same factor reproduces the painted panel: 0.18 × the Musou coating’s ~1 % gives the book value 0.177 %. One number now predicts any coating on this geometry. The right panel shows why the extruded trench also loses 2× on totals: diffuse bounces escape along its open axis; the pyramid’s fourth pair of walls closes them.

The paint term, priced (user: Musou Black 30,000 KRW per 100 ml): at an assumed effective coverage of 3–6 m²/L [coverage assumed, not measured — a coupon pins it], painting all 1,000 m² of slant surface costs 50M–100M KRW in paint alone — likely more than every other line item combined. Painting only the audience-critical 10–20 % of area cuts that to 5M–20M KRW, and the bare-black zones lose nothing a cheap paint could restore: an ordinary matte black (ρ ~4–5 %) lands at 0.18 × ρ ≈ the bare urethane itself, so on non-critical zones the rational choice is NO paint at all.

Bare-material bracket assumes black urethane/TPU at Lambertian ρ 0.04–0.08; one coupon pins the point on the 0.18 line.

9.2 — paint the tops only? Totals yes, head-on NO

The user proposed spraying only the tip region from the front (the top 5 mm of face is just 6 % of the paint area, so the 50–100M paint bill would drop 16×). Measured with the depth-split material (Musou above the plane, bare ρ 0.05 below; predictions registered in scripts/sweep_phase92.py, gradings in results/FINDINGS_phase92.md):

The two axes weight depth in OPPOSITE directions. Totals are earned at the top: at oblique incidence the flank shadows the cell, so lit area and sky view concentrate near the tips — 25 % of the paint bought 68 % of the totals gap. Head-on is earned everywhere, which means mostly at the bottom: at normal incidence nothing shadows, the beam lights the whole face down to the valley, the camera sees all of it, and the return is area-weighted — painting the cheap 6 % cannot move a number owned by the deep 94 % (the area average predicts the measured value to a few percent). Every registered prediction missed toward this law, and the registered ship rule (total ≤ 0.35 % AND head-on ≤ 0.06) failed on both counts: the front-spray tier does not ship. It survives only as a niche for zones that need tighter totals while their head-on is unseen.

9.4 — the fiber forest (flocking) fails head-on at 1.00

Flocking paper is a fiber forest on a 1 m roll with zero molding — if it worked, it would replace the whole non-critical cast tier. Modeled as Lambertian ρ 0.05 pillars (spacing 2 mm scale-invariant stand-in, fills 4–25 %, heights 10/20; predictions in scripts/sweep_phase94.py, gradings in results/FINDINGS_phase94.md): totals land at 1.08–1.87 % — above the bare pyramid’s 0.907 % — and head-on measured 1.0004 at the 7.5 mm beam: indistinguishable from a bare flat plate, the 9.2 area law verbatim (the camera-facing area of a pillar field IS its flat floor; the pyramid’s sloped bases leave no flat area at all). The registered replacement rule failed on both counts. One door stays open and is named: real fibers tilt and entangle, hiding the floor — not representable by vertical pillars, and exactly what the few-thousand-won flocking coupon measures.

The manufacturing decision as it now stands

Mold casting is the default: SLA positive master → platinum-silicone mold (peeled off the part, treated as a consumable) → soft urethane cast; row-strip molds are the fallback if tip tearing appears; a ground-steel master when volume grows. Injection stays rejected (valley radius + demolding, measured in Phase 6.6). Paint becomes two-tier: Musou only on audience-critical zones; bare black elsewhere at 5× the total but 10× better head-on than any flat wall, with zero paint. Extrusion is a niche for known-beam-direction zones only. Physical gates before ordering: cast one tile through the pipeline, magnify tips and valleys (tip ≤ 0.15 mm, valley < R0.1), bend-test the Musou film on primed urethane, and measure one black-urethane coupon’s ρ.
