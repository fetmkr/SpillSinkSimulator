Three rankings that disagree

Hundreds of laser projectors converge in mid-air to form an image. Every beam continues past it and lands on a wall, which paints a sharp, legible copy of the artwork. A panel has three jobs, and they are not the same job. Ranked on each separately, the same twelve designs come out in different orders — and the one you can buy off the shelf cuts total reflectance fivefold while leaving head-on brightness exactly where a bare painted plate leaves it.

Rebuilt 2026-08-14. The commercial-honeycomb builder stepped its cell lattice along the wrong two axes: no cell shared an edge with any neighbour and 30.1 % of the panel face was open channel straight down to the flat backing slab. Every honeycomb and every stacked number on this page was re-measured on geometry that tessellates, and the bought honeycomb moved from 2nd on total reflectance to {{COMB_RANK}}. Why three rankings

Darkest, least legible, and dimmest to a viewer are three different panels

Each line is one design, traced across all three rankings. Lines that move four places or more are drawn in amber; dashed lines are designs that cannot be manufactured at the feature size they were measured at, and are kept in view rather than deleted.

The honeycomb is the clearest case, and it is the one you can buy. {{COMB_RANK}} of {{NCAND}} on total reflectance and last on both other axes. Its smear is {{COMB_SMEAR}}× — the returned line comes back narrower than on a flat wall — and its head-on brightness is {{COMB_PEAK}} against a bare flat plate's {{FLATPEAK}}. On the axis a viewer actually experiences, the structure does nothing at all. Vertical cell walls trap light but never move it sideways, and head-on you are looking straight down each cell at the flat backing slab beneath it — the tube is new, the surface at the end of it is the flat plate again.

The bigger lever

One unmeasured coating property moves the answer 25×

Head-on brightness against the coating's specular roughness, everything else held. The dashed line is a bare flat plate of the same coating. Note the log axis.

This project pins roughness at 0.30 everywhere on one argument — that it is an interior optimum for total reflectance. That argument is about a different quantity and has never been checked against the other two axes. The flat-plate curve swings by a factor of 332 across the range, but that is the reference swinging, not a design: the cone itself moves 25×. For scale, the entire topology search spans 1.6×.

Corrections

What was wrong before this report

The flat-plate baseline was a typed constant, wrong by 5.4×

Every "structure buys N×" claim divided by 6.12%. No render in the repository ever produced that number. Measured properly it is {{FLATPCT}}%, so the structure buys about 5.3×, not 28×.

It came from a bare single-sided plane: with no volume the face normal is undetermined and the Fresnel term saturates. The giveaway was 45° and 60° reading identical to four decimals — a constant, not physics.

The previous headline compared parts eight times apart

One design used a 0.05 mm edge, the reference a 0.4 mm tip — in families whose return is dominated by the exposed feature. Matched, the ordering inverts and seven of nine families sit within 1.11×.

Matching feature size alone is still wrong: it assumes a common process. Bought honeycomb foil is 0.03–0.1 mm; a moulded cone tip is 0.4 mm. Every family now sits at what its own process delivers.

The blade array was measured as a wedge, not a sheet

The spec said 0.1 mm laser-cut sheet. The rendered part was 0.1 mm at the mouth thickening to 0.9 mm at the root. Re-measured at constant thickness the smear drops from 4.5× to 3.44× — the taper was making a quarter of it.

The geometry builder's default taper was the self-supporting direction for printing, left in place after the family was reclassified as sheet metal.

The honeycomb was an idealisation nobody sells

It was built as a Voronoi tessellation of jittered points, so every cell is a different shape. Real expanded honeycomb has identical cells, double-thickness walls where the foil ribbons are bonded, and is periodic by construction. Re-measured as the real product it reads {{COMB_PCT}}%.

Being periodic collides with this project's own rule against periodic arrays — a scanning beam over a regular lattice makes regular bright spots. That is a real property of the bought option, not something to design away.

Before anyone builds this

What this does not settle

Nothing here has been built or measured. The two coating properties that dominate all three rankings — the diffuse fraction and the specular roughness — are inputs that were chosen, not measured, and a flat plate's reflectance-versus-angle curve cannot constrain either. One printed and measured coupon settles both.

The closest published prior art (Kaster, J. Appl. Phys. 138, 174904, 2025, Carl Zeiss AG) is also simulation-only and fixes the same coating split without sweeping it. A granted patent (US 11,209,577 B2) covers slanted millimetre-scale macro structures in both periodic and irregular arrangements, with hexagonal, triangular, square and conical bases — while publishing no reflectance data at all.
