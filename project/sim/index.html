why beam width matters

Smear and span depend on beam÷pitch (phase 5.5), so the probe beam is a first-class input. 7.5 is the deployment default; set 2.0 only to reproduce legacy numbers. Real beam = aperture + divergence × distance; e.g. LaserCube Ultra (4 mm, 1 mrad) reads ≈9 mm at 5 m.

Reduced fidelity: 6 stripe phases at 256 spp, not the report's 16 at 512. An estimate of the published number, not the number.

Tools

A sample, not the measurement. The trace follows a few hundred rays at five angles in ONE azimuth plane at a time (turn φ to rotate it). The Render above reads its five θ in three planes (φ 0/45/90), and each render number integrates ALL the light leaving the panel with the real fitted coating, where the trace follows a few hundred rays with a single-ρ material.

ρ comes from the coating picked on the left; the trace scatters from that single number, not the full fitted mix. fitted uses the same diffuse-fraction and roughness sliders the Render uses, so the two can be compared directly. The other two modes are the pure pictures: all-diffuse, or all-mirror.

how to read the trace

path color = its measurement angle (same colors as the arrows) · red × = where the ray died (energy below one millionth; nothing is drawn past death, so every line leaving the panel is living light) · solid = arriving; dotted = after bouncing. Dots keep the ray's exact color and size — only the gaps widen as energy falls (each bounce keeps only ρ) · bars = bounce histogram, all angles together

per angle — note the DENOMINATORS differ: absorbed and out are shares of the INCIDENT beam, wander and retro are shares of what ESCAPES. When almost nothing escapes the last two are printed as —, because 100 % of nothing reads as 100 %. absorbed = how much of the entering light the wall destroys; out = the sliver that returns (→ 반사 총량); wander = the share of that sliver re-emerging more than half a beam width from where it entered (→ 모양 뭉개기); retro = the share fired straight back at the source (→ 정면 반짝임). Crude analogs for intuition — the Render buttons above are the measurement. φ rotates the approach around the panel; the render measures φ 0/45/90, so the dial is for aiming a trace anywhere between those planes.

The actual transport, not a render: rays are cast at the measured incidence, intersected against the same triangles, and scattered from a Lambertian at each hit. It uses its own scattering rule, so the figures illustrate the mechanism rather than second-guess Cycles.
