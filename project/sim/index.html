      —
      why beam width matters

      Smear and span depend on beam÷pitch (phase 5.5), so the probe beam is a first-class input. 7.5 is the deployment default; set 2.0 only to reproduce legacy numbers. Real beam = aperture + divergence × distance; e.g. LaserCube Ultra (4 mm, 1 mrad) reads ≈9 mm at 5 m.

      Reduced fidelity: 6 stripe phases at 256 spp, not the report's 16 at 512. An estimate of the published number, not the number.

      The two buttons above ask how much light comes back. This one asks where it goes. It puts the beam at each incidence angle in turn and looks from each observation angle in turn, and reads the BRDF — brightness per unit solid angle, in 1/sr — for every pair. 81 renders, one per cell.

      x = incidence, y = observation · — — retro, back at the projector · — — flat-mirror specular · —— the audience. Log scale. Hover a cell for its number.

      not measured
      —
      how to read the map

      Three questions are three straight lines. On y = +x the observer sits where the projector is, so that line is what fires back up the beam. On y = −x sits the direction a flat mirror would send it — a bright ridge there means the panel is only dimming the reflection, not moving it. y = 0 is the audience, and it is the one row a client actually stands in.

      Every cell is read against the flat Lambertian control plate in its own frame, so the number is an absolute BRDF and not a ratio to something unstated. A perfect Lambertian of ρ reads ρ/π everywhere — 0.0159 for the 5 % control — which is what the rig is gated against.

      What this cannot say. It is ONE azimuth plane, so there is no hemisphere total in it. And the coating fit constrains only the flat plate's total reflectance — nothing in it constrains BRDF shape, which is exactly what this map reads. The structure of the map is a geometric result; the absolute numbers wait on a measured coupon. See metrics/08.

      Tools

      A sample, not the measurement. The trace follows a few hundred rays at five angles in ONE azimuth plane at a time (turn φ to rotate it). The Render above reads its five θ in three planes (φ 0/45/90) and integrates ALL the light leaving the panel; the trace follows a few hundred rays in one plane, one scatter sample per bounce. Same coating, far fewer samples.

      The tracer carries ONE material, so it uses the one on the inner sides — about 94 % of the area, and where nearly every bounce happens. It owns all four numbers: ρ, the diffuse fraction, the roughness and the IOR. The tips and base finishes are in the render but not in this trace. coating scatters from the same Fresnel mix the render builds; specular and diffuse are its two EXTREMES, kept because a mirror ladder shows the mechanism a Monte Carlo cloud hides. They are not bounds on it: on honeycomb + flat base at anodised_hi, specular read 6.8x the measurement at θ 0 and 0.06x at θ 20.

      how to read the trace

      path color = its measurement angle (same colors as the arrows) · red × = where the ray died (energy below one millionth; nothing is drawn past death, so every line leaving the panel is living light) · solid = arriving; dotted = after bouncing. Dots keep the ray's exact color and size — only the gaps widen as energy falls (each bounce keeps only ρ) · bars = bounce histogram, all angles together

      per angle — note the DENOMINATORS differ: absorbed and out are shares of the INCIDENT beam, wander and retro are shares of what ESCAPES. When almost nothing escapes the last two are printed as —, because 100 % of nothing reads as 100 %. absorbed = how much of the entering light the wall destroys; out = the sliver that returns (→ 반사 총량); wander = the share of that sliver re-emerging more than half a beam width from where it entered (→ 모양 뭉개기); retro = the share fired straight back at the source (→ 정면 반짝임). Crude analogs for intuition — the Render buttons above are the measurement. φ rotates the approach around the panel; the render measures φ 0/45/90, so the dial is for aiming a trace anywhere between those planes.

      The actual transport, not a render: rays are cast at the measured incidence, intersected against the same triangles, and scattered from a Lambertian at each hit. It uses its own scattering rule, so the figures illustrate the mechanism rather than second-guess Cycles.
