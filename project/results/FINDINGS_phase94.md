## What the P1 inversion taught

My exposure model treated the floor as open area; the render says the
gaps between 20 mm pillars at 2 mm spacing are deep SLOTS — thinner
pillars widen the slots but they stay cavity-shaded, so totals came in
BELOW the band and lower fill measured lower totals (1.07 < 1.15 <
1.76 %). Totals are therefore not the forest's problem.

## The verdict is head-on, and it is total

At normal incidence the camera looks straight down the wells: the flat
floor at the bottom is fully lit and fully visible, and the field's
peak ratio measured **1.0004 — indistinguishable from a bare flat
plate**. The 9.2 area law called it: head-on is owned by whatever flat
area faces the camera, and a pillar field's camera-facing area IS its
floor. The pyramid's entire advantage on this axis is that its sloped
bases leave zero flat area anywhere.

Registered decision rule (total ≤ 0.9 % AND head-on ≤ 0.11): **FAILED
on both. Flocking cannot replace the bare cast tier.**

## The one door left open (named, unmodeled)

Real flock fibers are TILTED and entangled — the floor may be hidden
by fiber overlap in a way vertical pillars cannot represent. That is
the single mechanism that could rescue the material, it is not in this
model, and it is exactly what the 몇천 원 flocking-paper coupon
measures (modulated-beam protocol, one evening). Until that coupon
says otherwise, the two-tier cast plan stands unchanged.
